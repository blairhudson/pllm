    The verification procedure does not impose any additional overhead on the
servers. The client’s work is dominated by a single multi-exponentiation, which
incurs a cost of (m + n)/ log(m + n) group exponentiations. For large enough
m, n, this is significantly less than the cost of the O(mn) arithmetic operations
needed to compute the matrix-vector product. More importantly, the memory
requirements of the client are reduced from O(mn) to O(m + n).

(Note that the {Si } can be written in terms of the {Ri } given M.) In the original
setting of Freivalds’s algorithm finding such a d is (information-theoretically)
