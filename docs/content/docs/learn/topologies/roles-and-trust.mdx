---
title: "Roles and trust"
description: "Distinguish graph instances, independent operators, and declared privacy assumptions."
---

Role names describe work, not operator independence. In the public prepared
path, the client keeps prompts, activations, masks, tokenizer and persistent
state. Preparation receives stage seeds offline, computes masked corrections,
and must erase its masks. Inference holds the public transformer body and
consumes one-use masked stage requests online. Preparation and Inference must
follow the protocol and not collude; a local process pair does not establish
that separation.

The role graph lists each directed offline or online channel. Its
`separate_operators` pairs are **requirements**. The inspector reports whether
a declared placement violates them; labels alone cannot verify real ownership
or establish a cryptographic proof. One-use material still burns on failure,
cancellation or replay. Protected regions must cover every operation before
they can move from the client to another role.

An alternative two-online-server offset comparator gives independent servers
fresh additive input shares and has the client reconstruct each linear result.
Its protocol and placement require separate review and execution; merely
declaring a two-server graph does not activate it. See
[traffic and compute](/learn/topologies/network-and-compute/) for the matched
comparison and [TEE trust](/learn/topologies/trusted-execution/) for a different
hardware-backed assumption.
