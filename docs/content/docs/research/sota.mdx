## What the tiers mean

| Maximum | Intended client | Tradeoff to explore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low · 256 MiB** | Minimal client worker | Paging, indexed tokenization, tiled prefill and bounded caches |
| **Medium · 2 GiB** | Application worker, including ECS tasks, Kubernetes pods and ordinary instances | Selective residency, prefix reuse and fewer exchanges |
| **High · 16 GiB** | Compute-rich worker, optionally GPU-enabled | Larger working sets, concurrency and supported device acceleration |

The ceiling covers the **whole client process**, not just model tensors. Tier
eligibility requires every recorded fresh-client peak to fit; metric columns
retain their original single-sample or median summaries. Unknown RAM cannot
qualify. Cold/repeat scenarios retain their complete process-lifetime peak.
An empty tier means no qualifying measurement under those selected conditions.
It does not prove the workload impossible.

GPU memory has its own capacity requirements; High does not select a GPU.
Process RSS also excludes some container-charged file cache and device memory.
Measurements qualify only their recorded model, workload, concurrency and
lifecycle, rather than guaranteeing deployment admission.

The network-configured SDK accepts `OpenAI(max_client_memory="low")`,
`"medium"` or `"high"`. The same values work in `PlanningPolicy` and
`[client.planning]`. Live memory checks can tighten a chosen maximum; omitting
the maximum keeps automatic headroom-based admission. See
[maximum client RAM in the SDK](/sdk/run/deployment/#maximum-client-ram).

## Exact boundary decode screen

Fifteen fresh Qwen2.5 clients compare serial/parallel head dispatch and u32/exact
uniform-residue replies at 150+8 tokens under shared native 100/40 caps. All
131,404,800 logit/KV values and complete state/output commitments match; observed
swap growth is zero. Every configuration fits observed Low and Medium.

| Head choice | N−1 decode tokens/s | Change vs serial/u32 | Largest complete-client peak |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Serial/u32 | 2.182 | Control | 221.08 MiB |
| Parallel/u32 | 3.045 | +39.53% | 222.83 MiB |
| Serial/residues | 2.458 | +12.64% | 229.47 MiB |
| Parallel/residues | **3.326** | **+52.42%** | **228.67 MiB** |

The combined choice saves exactly **2,125,920 head-reply bytes**. Against
serial/u32, total TCP streams fall 0.58%, client CPU rises 3.60% and median client
RSS rises 1.20%. Against the fresh maintained prepared comparator, traffic falls
**23.70%** and full request latency **24.19%**, while decode remains **32.78%
slower**. Complete client RAM includes SDK, supervisor, dashboard, relay and audit.

## Cold-request tier confirmation

Twenty-four fresh Qwen2.5 clients compare seven prepared incumbents with the
coupled private lookup/full head at 150+8 tokens under shared native 100/40 caps.
Complete client ownership includes the SDK, supervisor, dashboard, relay and
exact-state audit. All eight recipes fit observed **Low (256 MiB)** and therefore
**Medium (2 GiB)**; the coupled candidate's largest replicate is **210.22 MiB**.

Against the best eligible tested incumbent for each metric:

| Metric | Coupled boundary change |
| --- | ---: |
| All-link TCP streams | **−19.93%** |
| Full cold request latency | **−20.26%** |
| Target startup-through-cleanup time | **−10.88%** |
| Client cold CPU | −2.42% median; observed ranges overlap |
| Client lifetime peak RSS | +12.04% |
| Online TTFT | +25.25% |

Every checked logit/KV value, position, selection, RNG commitment and output
matches; observed swap growth is zero. The added two boundary workers retain a

is source-present: checkpoint/tokenizer distribution and physical wire remain

private token lookup and the complete tied head run at two boundary workers while
the original prepared decoder body stays remote. Full vocabulary, logits, KV,
history and sampling behavior are preserved. The earlier three-pair Qwen2.5

## Prepared combination screen

Thirty fresh Qwen2.5 clients completed all sixty cold/repeat responses at 150/288
input tokens plus eight outputs. Compared with the matched maintained control:

| Combination | Client lifetime RAM | Preparation cold CPU | Cold online TTFT | Cold + repeat TCP traffic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 64-row tiling + digit8 | −8.79% / −13.54% | −24.84% / −34.75% | +3.75% / +4.21% | Essentially unchanged |
| Above + selective placement | −8.93% / −13.59% | −28.72% / −39.14% | −4.84% / −4.26% | −4.51% / −8.26% |

Each pair is **150 / 288 input tokens**, within its own workload. Selective
placement keeps at least 90% of body linear MACs remote. Its client cold CPU rises
5.54% / 1.31%, and complete cold requests change +1.79% / −5.59%. Tiling alone has
the lowest observed client RAM. Outputs match and observed swap growth is zero.
These are **screen leaders**, pending independent finalist confirmation. Select
“Five-way prepared combinations” above for all choices and separate cold/repeat
timings; the [full analysis](https://github.com/blairhudson/pllm/blob/main/docs/research/sota-combinations-reference.md)
retains all seven measured priority metrics and traffic reconciliation.

## Reading the table

Comparisons use one checked cohort at a time: matching source, numeric contract,
workload, output identity and measurement lifecycle. Separate invocations, changed
numerics and different topologies keep their own leaders. Sample counts and
measurement windows remain explicit; these observations are not confidence intervals or an exhaustive search
of every possible combination. Quality and privacy depend on the linked contracts.

Base and output-dependent traffic require an attributed ledger, including prepaid
and unused output material. Complete headline rates stay **Unattributed** when
evidence is missing or partial. The new screen's expandable **application-body
traffic** details show known base, known output-dependent, residual and total bytes
for each replicate. TCP totals and whole-response averages cannot fill those gaps.
Available public artifact sizes appear beside the combination; their distribution
remains outside response counters.
